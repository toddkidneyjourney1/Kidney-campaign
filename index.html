Living Kidney Donor

Finding a living donor is my highest priority — the fastest path to transplant with the best long-term outcomes. You don't need to be a blood relative.

What does donation involve?

Donors need to be in good physical and mental health, free from major chronic conditions. Blood type is no longer a hard barrier — compatibility is determined through testing. Screening takes 4–8 weeks and is fully confidential. Most donors return to normal activities within 4–6 weeks.

Frequently Asked Questions

Everything you need to know about kidney donation.

Blood type compatibility is no longer a barrier thanks to modern paired-exchange programs at HCA Florida Largo. Many donors with different blood types can participate through innovative matching systems.

A living donor can provide a kidney that often lasts longer and functions immediately. The waitlist for a deceased-donor kidney can be 3–5 years or more, making living donation the most effective and timely path to saving my life.

No. All medical expenses for a living donor's evaluation, surgery, and follow-up care are covered by the recipient's insurance or the transplant center. Donors do not pay out-of-pocket for medical costs.

Living donors are screened extensively to ensure they are healthy enough to donate. Studies show that kidney donors have the same life expectancy as the general population. The remaining kidney increases its capacity to filter blood effectively, maintaining normal function.

Visit the HCA Florida Largo screening portal. The process is completely confidential and you can stop at any time without pressure or obligation.
